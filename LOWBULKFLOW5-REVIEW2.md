## 1. Summary of findings

1. **The receiver's advertised window and its actual hold are in different units, and the hold is about three times smaller than the window.** The advertisement is `min(ReceiveQueueMaxByteCount, leaf total)` in payload bytes [S transfer.go:12446-12452], but on mobile the leaf is charged in retained units: carrier root + one pooled root per decoded frame + a 1 KiB owner [S transfer.go:1414-1447, frame_protobuf.go:1018]. Measured exactly (Appendix B): an 8×1,100-byte Pack costs 26,624 retained bytes for 8,800 payload bytes (3.03×); a 3×1,100 Pack 11,264 for 3,300 (3.41×); a single ~760-byte Pack ≈5,120 for 760 (6.7×). So the Pixel32 leaf of 2,181,038 B holds roughly 0.72 MB of payload (8-frame geometry) while advertising 2,181,038 B. Source states the design invariant that a sender keeping to the advertisement "can never force an eviction whatever the gap structure" [S transfer.go:12440-12445]; this invariant does not hold on mobile. The device evidence fits this mechanism in both valid runs: receive-queue refusals start in the same 5-second batch in which the leaf reaches its cap, and throughput collapses afterwards (Section 3.1). I rank this above the brief's four candidate mechanisms as the first thing to test; it is receiver-local, needs no wire change and no memory increase.

2. **The prepared 4 MiB / 8 MiB leaf candidate cannot raise the advertisement by more than ~28% and leaves the unit mismatch in place.** On the device the per-sequence field `ReceiveQueueMaxByteCount` is 2,796,202 B (32 profile) / 5,592,405 B (64 profile) [A, from S], so with the larger leaf the field binds: advertisement 2,181,038 → 2,796,202 (32) and 4,362,076 → 5,592,405 (64). The hold in payload terms grows to ~1.39 MB / ~2.77 MB (8-frame), still about half the advertisement. The brief's own rejection criterion ("fewer drops without improved throughput") is the predicted outcome. Separately, the host constructor receipts show `receive_queue_max = 2,621,440` at both 32 and 64 [E], which is the unscaled Connect default and is only consistent with a harness process whose `MemoryBudget()` is unset; the phone sets it [S sdk.go:568, MainApplication.kt:1121]. The host screen therefore does not reproduce the device's advertisement (Section 4).

3. **On the provider send side the retained charge is roughly 3.6× payload because each item is charged three roots plus an owner** [S transfer_retained_budget.go:63-80]. For the SDK64 synthetic provider (send budget 2,876,094 B [E]) that bounds payload in flight at ~0.80 MB (8-frame) / ~0.66 MB (3-frame). The host medians 131 / 98 Mbps imply an effective round trip of about 48 / 54 ms against a configured 40 ms path plus a 10 ms ACK-compression residence [S transfer.go:768] — i.e. the host numbers are consistent with a flight-bound sender, and the eight-frame gain is partly a charge-efficiency gain (4.36× → 3.62×). The smallest provider-side intervention is to charge the two scratch roots once per sequence rather than per item (Section 6, request 3). This is [H] until the RTT sweep the brief already proposes is run.

4. **The device diagnostics cannot resolve the brief's central questions because they do not record the advertised window, the sender window estimate, RTT/ACK age, Pack geometry or memory-wait time.** I listed every field in the 5-second diagnostic parts (Appendix C); none of these exist. Dropped-Pack size can be inferred: `ReceiveQueueDropByteCount / ReceiveQueueDropCount` = 762 B (Pixel32) and 760 B (Pixel64 Fast1) [A], which says the refused Packs were small on average, not 8-frame Packs. Request 5 lists the minimal additions.

5. **Pixel64's second half is not a capacity story.** From Fast2 +35 s the speed destinations' counters freeze (drops stop at 26,205; the client's own writes to them rise by only ~30 over the next 150 s), transfer root use falls to 1.3 MB, and nothing moves in Fast3 [E]. The platform transport budget was at its slot cap (16 of 16 transports, `transport_budget_pending_h1 = 1`) before Fast1 and again during late Fast2 [E]; a new H1 transport claim waits when the cap is reached [S platform_transport_budget.go:554-566]. That is a concrete, measurable candidate for the "fresh provider never admitted" symptom on the device and is not in the brief [H].

6. **The brief's numbers are reproducible; several inferences and labels are not.** Per-run drop/gap/GC "brackets" overlap by one batch at each boundary (the Pixel32 "851" is inside the "2,994"); the Pixel64 drop counts are speed-destination-only (all-destination totals 30,252 / 11,749 / 0); "local timeout resends were zero" is false for Pixel64 (speed destination +103 timeout-resend writes, +398 deferrals in Fast1); the 32/64 destination sets do intersect at the window level (6 shared of 11/20); the "eight-frame treatment" is HEAD code and the "three-frame" arm is older source, so "candidate" there means current production; unknown/legacy H1 routes are not "three-frame" but 16 frames bounded at 3 KiB → 3,300 B; the 100-page "99 unfinished handoffs" is the file's `overlapProofs`; and in the outage test the CSS witnesses `css_original_tuple`, `css_held_through_html`, `css_public_owner_retained` are all false, so "preserving the original 12 KiB CSS connection" overstates what was shown. Details in Section 3.

7. **Newer runs the brief does not cite change the recovery status.** `D/final-publication-qualified.k_12wkf0` ran 32 roots: 28 pass, 4 fail; `D/refresh-v2-fixed-qualified.l462hdfp` ran 39: 37 pass, 2 fail (the same two remote-refusal lifecycle tests), with the full-window causal root passing under the fix [E]. Both directories were complete when read (newest files 14:39 and 14:42). The brief's statement that the complete fixes are unqualified remains true, but the fail set is now known. Also: the one FAIL→PASS smoke the brief credits to a "preliminary publication fix" ran the superseded synchronous all-flow-scan predecessor, not the bounded successor (`SOURCE-ADMISSION.json` of the smoke names the earlier packet) [E].

8. **The harness confounder is confirmed and slightly worse than described.** `closeTarget`'s `success` is never read, the Go loop discards the child exit code and reads no receipt field, an existing test asserts the current retain-all-children behaviour, no `Target.getTargets` call exists anywhere, and all three Fast runs share one browser context (Section 3.6).

### Ranked next steps

1. **Deterministic test of the hold/advertisement invariant** (receiver-only, Connect unit test): a mobile-policy `ReceiveSequence` with retained accounting and a sender that fills exactly the advertised window behind a single head gap must record zero `ReceiveQueueDropCount` / `ReceiveQueueEvictionCount`. Expected on HEAD: refusals after ~1/3 of the window. Then the smallest fix: make `receiveWindowAdvertisement` return `min(field, leafTotal × payloadPerRetainedByte)` with the ratio measured from the queue's own held items (floor at a constant such as 1/4 for an empty queue). Reject if, with the fix, drops under a 0.5% loss stratum do not fall to near zero or loaded-probe latency worsens.
2. **Provider send-side charge**: change `retainedMemoryByteCount` to one root per item plus a per-sequence scratch reserve of two roots; verify with the RTT sweep (40/80/120 ms, same provider and budgets) that throughput is inverse in RTT on HEAD and that the intercept moves with the charge. Reject if throughput does not move with RTT on HEAD (then it is not flight-bound).
3. **Device diagnostics** (request 5): add advertised window, received advertisement, `SendWindowEstimate.{Window,Ceiling,Reason}`, retained-vs-payload ratio of the held queue, Pack geometry histogram, memory-wait count/duration, transport-slot wait duration. All counters; no payload.
4. **Harness gate**: stop a Fast run when the prior receipt has `cleanupComplete=false`, read `closeTarget.success`, enumerate targets before each run (Section 3.6 gives the test shape). Do this before another physical run is used as evidence.
5. **Recovery fixes**: finish the two failing remote-refusal lifecycle tests before re-running the 100-page replay; add the transport-slot pending wait to the outage trace to test finding 5.
6. **Re-run the RX-leaf candidate only after 1 and 3**, with the advertisement recorded per arm; otherwise it measures a moving target.

## 5. Answers to the six review requests

### Request 1 — Rank the four mechanisms and name the one measurement that separates each pair

Ranking by current evidence weight for the device result (not by prior plausibility):

1. **Client receive hold versus advertisement (a specific form of "client RX leaf")** — device-evidenced time order in both valid runs (3.1); source-derived mechanism (finding 1); wasted carrier bytes 22–84% [A]. Distinct from "make the leaf bigger": the defect is the unit mismatch, and it persists at any leaf size unless the advertisement is derived from the hold.
2. **Provider retained flight** — host-evidenced by arithmetic (finding 3), not device-evidenced (no provider counters). Governs the ceiling once drops are removed; for a 64 MiB mobile-style provider the ceiling is ~0.8 MB / effective RTT.
3. **Reverse reliable ACK recovery** — device-evidenced as degradation, not collapse: Pixel32 Fast3 had +763 gap-repair writes and `AckRouteWriteMaxWait` 7 → 24 ms with zero incoming drops and still delivered 18 Mbps [E]. Its effect on the provider's inner-TCP ACK clock is unmeasured.
4. **Fresh-connection recovery** — deterministic component failures proven; device link is the transport-slot cap hypothesis (finding 5) and the Fast2 +35 s freeze; not shown causal for Fast2's timeout.

Separating measurements (one per pair, cheapest first):

| Pair | Missing measurement |
| --- | --- |
| 1 vs 2 | Per-sequence sample of `Σ queueByteCount / Σ messageByteCount` of held items alongside the advertised window on the client, and `resendQueue` used-vs-payload-outstanding on the provider, at the same 5 s cadence. Drops that start when the client ratio × used reaches the leaf while provider admission has headroom isolate 1. |
| 1 vs 3 | Direction-labelled time series already exist (`ReceiveQueueDropCount` incoming; `SelectiveGapWriteCount` outgoing). Add the provider-side receipt counter of our ACK Packs (count and max inter-arrival). Gap writes rising while the provider's receipt is continuous means reverse loss is local; 1 is silent when drops are zero. |

### Request 2 — The constant-root 4 / 8 MiB RX intervention

Evidence class: the diff is read [E]; its effect on the advertisement is arithmetic [A, A.3]; the throughput effect is a hypothesis [H].

- **First deterministic failure test (before touching the leaf size).** Connect unit test: build a `ReceiveSequence` with the mobile receive settings (`ReceiveQueueRetainedByteAccounting=true`, `ReceiveQueueMinByteCount=0`, a parented leaf of 2,181,038 B, `ReceiveQueueMaxByteCount=2,796,202`) and a sender that delivers 8-frame 1,100-byte Packs with sequence number 1 withheld and bytes 2..N filling exactly `receiveWindowAdvertisement()` of payload. Assert `ReceiveQueueDropCount == 0 && ReceiveQueueEvictionCount == 0`. Expected on HEAD: refusals once ~26,624 × k exceeds the leaf, i.e. after about 82 Packs (~0.72 MB) of the advertised 2.18 MB. Repeat with 3-frame and single-frame Packs.
- **Smallest intervention.** Not the leaf size. Derive the advertisement from the hold: `min(ReceiveQueueMaxByteCount, leafTotal × r)` where `r` is the queue's running payload-per-retained-byte ratio (held items' `messageByteCount / queueByteCount`, with a conservative floor such as 1/4 when empty). No wire change; legacy senders unaffected; memory unchanged. A second, independent intervention is to reduce the retained ratio itself (e.g. release the carrier root once frames are copied out, which the decoder already does [S frame_protobuf.go:1018]) — that is a memory-ownership change and needs the custody audit the brief requires.
- **Measurements.** Advertised window (new field), `ReceiveQueueDropCount/ByteCount`, `ReceiveQueueEvictionCount`, held-queue ratio, `PackHandoffWaitCount`, `pool_retained_bytes`, allocation per useful byte, loaded-probe TTFB p95, upload throughput, cancellation drain time — per arm, five consecutive windows, AB/BA/AB as the brief's plan already states, at loss strata 0 and 0.005.
- **Distinguishing improved credit from moved congestion or garbage.** (a) Report the advertisement per arm; with the leaf change alone it must read +28.2%, not +92%/+100%; if it reads unchanged the harness lacks a process budget. (b) Under the 0.005 loss stratum, drops must fall in proportion to the hold increase (×1.9 for the leaf change, to ~0 for the advertisement fix); drops unchanged with a larger leaf means the refusals are count-bound or handoff-bound, not byte-bound. (c) `pool_retained_bytes` and allocation per useful byte must not rise faster than throughput; if they do the gain is garbage. (d) `PackHandoffWaitCount` must not rise; if it does the pressure moved to the handoff channel (same budget size, same mismatch).
- **Rejection.** Reject the leaf-size candidate if, at 0.005 loss, median throughput over five windows does not improve by at least the fraction of carrier bytes currently wasted (22% on Pixel32-like loss), or if loaded-probe p95 TTFB worsens by more than one ACK-compression interval, or if drops do not fall. Reject the advertisement fix if drops fall to ~0 but throughput falls below HEAD at zero loss (that would mean the honest window is the binder and the next step is the provider-side flight or Pack geometry).
- **Guards the candidate already has** [E `PLAN.json`]: head-gap recovery, upload-and-control progress, blocked-upload cancellation, role transitions, sibling budgets. **Missing:** a test that asserts hold ≥ advertisement in payload terms, a loss stratum in the wire controls, and a harness `SetMemoryBudget` matching the device so the field binds as it does on the phone.

### Request 3 — A provider-side intervention with precise ownership release and stable older-peer behaviour

Evidence class: charge formula [S transfer_retained_budget.go:63-80]; exact values [Appendix B]; host-fit arithmetic [A.5]; throughput effect [H].

- **First deterministic failure test.** `SendSequence` with a lifetime resend budget of 2,876,094 B and a receiver advertising ≥ 2.6 MB, zero loss, 8-frame Packs: assert that payload outstanding reaches at least 90% of `min(advertisement, ResendQueueMaxByteCount-derived window)` before any `ErrSendPackNotAdmitted`/memory wait. Expected on HEAD: admission stops at ~0.80 MB payload (90 items × 31,812 B) while the window estimate still permits more (`ceiling = LendableByteCount` in retained units [S transfer.go:12702]).
- **Smallest intervention.** Charge the two "scratch roots for decoding and rewriting a full head/contract" once per sequence (a per-sequence reserve of two roots taken at sequence start) instead of per item: per-item charge 9,580 + 3,072 = 12,652 B for an 8-frame Pack (1.44×) versus 31,812 (3.62×). Ownership release points are unchanged (item release on ACK; the sequence reserve on close). Older peers see no wire difference. The legacy (v1) branch keeps its extra roots. Keep `retainedSendPackFits` and the grant path as they are.
- **Matched pairs.** Same provider process build, same 40 ms RTT, AB/BA/AB with fresh processes, five consecutive 10-second windows, loaded 16 KiB probes under load (already collected in the three-pair harness), explicit cancellation and owner joins at the end of each arm, plus an **RTT sweep at 40 / 80 / 120 ms on HEAD first**: if median throughput is inverse in RTT and the product throughput × RTT ≈ 0.8 MB, the sender is flight-bound by the retained admission and the intervention should raise the product toward the advertised window.
- **Measurements.** Per sequence: payload outstanding, `resendQueue` used (retained), `SendWindowEstimate.{Window,Ceiling,Reason}`, memory-wait count/duration (add; today only `ReliableAdmissionWait*` exists and measures a different gate), `RouteUnacknowledgedDuration`, inner-TCP `returnByteCount` per flow, origin read bytes; process RSS/Go live bytes to confirm the real allocation per item (if actual live roots per item are not three, the budget was over-charging and no memory is added; if they are, the change raises retention and must be rejected under the 64 MiB gate).
- **Rejection.** Reject if throughput on HEAD does not scale with RTT (not flight-bound); reject the change if Go live bytes per outstanding payload byte rise by more than the charge reduction predicts, if loaded-probe p95 worsens, or if any owner fails to join at cancellation.

### Request 4 — Bounded full-window replacement and publication-driven retained-SYN retry

Evidence class: candidate source trees diffed against HEAD and read [E/S, candidate file:line below are in the candidate trees under `C`]; both candidates were compiled and vetted with a Go overlay from the scratch directory (clean); no candidate test was run.

**Provenance correction first.** The "preliminary publication fix" that turned `TestDiscoveryDemandLatePublicationRetriesRetainedNativeSyn` from FAIL to PASS (`D/fixed-syn-publication-smoke.evqzk_kq`) ran the *predecessor* `native-syn-publication.SOURCEONLY.gui7xfxp` — its `SOURCE-ADMISSION.json` names that packet, and that tree still iterates `ip4PathUpdates`/`ip6PathUpdates` synchronously inside the admission callback and has no `SynRetryPublicationBatchSize` [E]. The r2 successor the brief lists (`5024ad45`) has no runtime result of its own except the later `final-publication-qualified` run (28/32). The brief's sentence "One preliminary publication fix changes its test from FAIL to PASS; the complete fixes remain unqualified" is therefore right, but the pass belongs to the design the brief says must be replaced.

**Shared base.** Both candidates sit on a small common change: `tcpSynRetry` gains a bounded `refusedRemoteIds` list plus a sticky `remoteRefusalOverflow`, paid inside the existing `retainSynRetry` reservation, recorded only for *signaled* refusals, capacity race width × window count + 1 (new leaf `ip_remote_multi_client_syn_retry_remote_refusal.go`, 61 lines). This is the "bounded failed-remote-tail history".

**Full-window refresh V2 (`C/full-window-service-refresh-v2.SOURCEONLY.wk0ndx1e`).** It does what the brief describes:
- Destination-scoped, exact-owner reservation of an idle failed channel: `requestServiceRefresh` (`ip_remote_multi_client_service_refresh.go:24-105`) qualifies only an unbound flow whose paid SYN owner has at least one signaled refusal and no overflow, and only when every offered member of the window has refused this SYN's remote tail (64-78); the victim is one of the refusing channels, from a strict, cooperative, non-degraded window exactly at `WindowSizeHardMax`, outside the discovery backoff (`serviceRefreshCapacity`, 162-172).
- O(1) custody and ABA safety: per-channel `serviceRefreshRaceReferences` and `serviceRefreshReservation` atomics; every race registration takes a reference (`tryServiceRefreshRaceReference`, 311-319; call sites `ip_remote_multi_client.go` 8134-8143, 8300-8317, 10017), the reference moves to `update.pendingServiceRefreshBinding` on winner selection and is released only after `bindClientFlow` publishes the flow (354-360, called at 4479 under the parent lock); the claim CASes `nil → owner` and the swap CASes `owner → retiring sentinel` (`strict_handover.go:371-374`), which can never be released or reused. Idle = `len(clientUpdates[victim]) == 0 && references == 0` under parent and victim locks (136-141).
- Existing retry authority and backoff respected (170-171, 178-179; `noteDiscoveryClientCount` compensation at 14358-14362; `serviceRefreshConnected` clears the expand retry only on a real answer, 288-301).
- Mint budget: `admitBudget, requestCount = 1, 1` per pass (14668-14670), one refresh per source generation (`serviceRefreshRequested`, cleared only by a new SYN sequence at 11750).
- Alias barrier: listing exclusions from `owner.refusedRemoteIds` (243-257) and final admission re-asks the original owner (`serviceRefreshCandidateAllowed`, 272-284, used at 14710-14715); `TestFullServiceRefreshRefusesFreshLocalAliasOfFailedRemote` covers it.
- V1 → V2: confirmed. V1 selected the send-only wide-race winner at 8405 and immediately closed the race, dropping the winner's reference before `bindClientFlow`; V2 adds the transfer at 8406-8409 (the only production hunk in `V2-ONLY.diff`) and a test that gates the winner before bookkeeping.

Defects and risks in V2 I would want closed before runtime qualification:
1. **Affinity-placed new flows are invisible to the idle test.** `adoptAffinityClientWithLock → inheritAffinityClient` (`affinity_time.go:121-137`) writes `update.client` under the parent lock with no race state and no reference; `clientUpdates` is populated later by `bindClientFlow` after the first send callback. In that interval `claimServiceRefreshOwner` can reserve a channel that already owns a flow. Mitigations exist (later affinity placements refuse a reserved channel, `affinity_time.go:167`; `takeStrictHandover` re-reads `flowCount(victim) != 0`, `strict_handover.go:334`), but that re-read is before `strictHandoverSwappableWithLock` (363) and not under the lock that `bindClientFlow` takes, so a bind landing between the two closes a victim with a live flow. The "newly established owner" tests cover race winners only. **First deterministic failure test:** affinity-place a flow on the would-be victim, hold its first send in the `beforeServiceRefreshBindForTest` hook, run the refresh pass, assert the claim fails (mirror of `SendOnlyWinnerBeforeBookkeepingIsNotIdle`).
2. **Victim choice is map-order arbitrary and the victim is closed before the newcomer answers** (84-88; `victim.Close(); removeClients(victim)` in `admitCandidate` ~14845). Under a persistently refused destination this retires a healthy idle exit once per app connect attempt (the flag resets with each new ISN). Not a leak; it is identity churn paced by five refusals plus backoff. The brief's own constraint — do not globally poison an exit because one destination refuses it — is respected, but a good exit is still cancelled on one destination's account. Smallest change: prefer the lowest-quality idle refusing member and keep the victim until the newcomer's first inbound, or cap refreshes per destination per interval.
3. **Attempted-but-never-listed refreshes consume the generation's one chance** (`serviceRefreshAttempted` set before `expand`, 14435-14439; `finishServiceRefresh` keeps the flag, 231-233). Conservative, not unsafe.
4. **New lock edge** flow-lock → channel-lock (`tryServiceRefreshRaceReference` under `update.stateLock`). I found no reverse path; the READY does not mention it; a `-race` run is required.
5. **Composition dependence**: V2 admits the late provider but nothing re-sends the retained SYN to it; `receivedInbound` stays false so the backoff advances on a successful admission until the app's next retransmit. Only with candidate B does `clientAdded` fire the retry. The two must be qualified together for the 100-page replay.

Request-4 cases with no V2 test: affinity-placement race (above); FIN/RST or a new SYN on the same 4-tuple while a refresh is installed or mid-pass; source cancellation during the running expand pass (the cancel test closes the flow before any provider exists); victim dying while reserved; last-resort rebind preempting a reservation through `removeClient`; fairness between two destinations or two window types contending for the single refresh slot; backoff clear on a real answer; memory-credit exhaustion (refused `retainSynRetry` or overflow yields no refresh and no leaked reference); any `-race` run.

**Bounded publication successor (`C/native-syn-publication-settings-r2.SOURCEONLY.__5dis_p`).** Also does what the brief says: `clientAdded` (`prober.go:831`) only links the admitted channel into a parent-lock FIFO coalesced by `client.synPublicationQueued` and wakes the existing reaper (`syn_retry_publication.go:9-27`); the index is an intrusive list of paid `tcpSynRetry` owners inserted after `retainSynRetry` releases the flow lock and unlinked from `releaseSynRetryMemory` before the wipe (31-77); the reaper visits at most `SynRetryPublicationBatchSize` (default 32) owners per turn and self-wakes (102-154); per-owner eligibility requires an unbound flow, a signaled refusal, publisher not in the refused tail (alias barrier, 181) and not in `dialFailedClients`, and a real race vacancy (185-196); dispatch reuses `queueSynRetryWithLock`, so **no new memory credit is needed** — credit exhaustion cannot strand a queued retry (if the original reservation was refused there is no owner). Closed publisher, cancelled source, FIN/RST before retry and a new SYN on the same tuple are each handled by existing validity checks (`IsDone`, `validWithLock`, generation number) and are tested with real fixtures (answer, new-SYN, owner close, FIN, RST, parent close, wrong family, warned/closed publisher, one vacancy only, leaf contention, retired alias).

Defects and risks in B:
1. **Work per publication is O(number of paid SYN owners), not constant**; the batch bounds only the per-turn slice, and `windowStatsInto` runs per visit rather than per event (85-87). Under a site-wide outage with many unanswered SYNs each admission re-walks them all.
2. **The reaper blocks on a busy leaf** (`Lock/Unlock` before `TryLock`, 165-166); HEAD's `drainSynRetries` has the same property, so this is not a regression but it is not "one attempt per work unit".
3. **The retry is not pinned to the publisher**: an empty race re-races over `orderedClients` filtered by service failures only (`ip_remote_multi_client.go:8461`), so aged-out refusers may be re-offered. Wasteful, not incorrect.

Request-4 cases with no B test: memory-credit exhaustion with a queued event; source cancellation via the SendSequence closing (`affinitySources.closed`); many real paid owners across several turns for one publication (multi-turn only with synthetic owners); fairness between two real publications; same-identity replacement through `expand`'s replace path; backoff non-interaction; any `-race` run.

**HEAD behaviour the candidates change** [S]: discovery demand exists only while an expansion pass has begun an acquisition and returns without a read once `targetSize <= offeredCount` (`ip_remote_multi_client_discovery_demand.go:185-191, 444-446`); `clientAdded` only wakes the prober (`ip_remote_multi_client_prober.go:827-831`); the only producer of a SYN retry is the first signaled dial failure (`ip_remote_multi_client.go:10230-10232`).

**Measurements and rejection for both.** Run the composed pair under `-race` on the 39-root and 32-root selectors; the two common remote-refusal lifecycle tests that fail today (retired local pointer retained by ordinary failure lists; new SYN inheriting a retired generation's refusal) must pass or be shown to be fixture defects. Then the all-initial outage 100-page root with the four snapshots extended by one at failure/success and a per-destination SYN/dial timeline. Reject if page 1 TTFB after the tap exceeds one retransmit interval of the late provider's admission time, if any healthy CSS-carrying owner is warned or cancelled, if more than one identity is minted per refusal generation, or if `-race` reports on the new flow-lock → channel-lock edge.

### Request 6 — Missing realism beyond the five-window and 100-page tests

- **Memory budget parity.** The host harness runs with an unset `MemoryBudget()` (constructor receipts), so `ReceiveQueueMaxByteCount`, the advertisement, `MemoryScaledByteCount` defaults and the transfer share all differ from the phone. - **RTT and loss.** All throughput work is one same-host provider at 40 ms and zero loss. Add 80/120 ms and 0.1–0.5% loss in each direction separately (reverse-only loss tests mechanism 3).
- **Multi-client provider.** One client per provider today; the provider's retained budgets are shared across clients in deployment.

Keep warm/cold windows (the three-pair file already separates `cold_window` from `windows_2_to_5`).

- **Not a win by itself.** Lower retransmit counters, fewer drops, or constructor-receipt checks passing do not count without the five-window median and the loaded-probe p95 moving in the right direction under the same arm.
