# Network performance research impact, 2026-08-24–2026-10-24

This is a timeline of the significant measured work in Connect, SDK and the server packet path over the last two months. It distinguishes local component improvements, end-to-end observations, functional recoveries and experiments that were rejected or never qualified. Dates refer to the research records, which sometimes differ from the eventual merge date. The latest private results are identified separately.

There is **no comparable single PERFVAR/MEMSTEADY/LOWBAR score** across this period. PERFVAR records scenario-specific throughput, latency, wire/retransmission cost, correctness and calibration; MEMSTEADY records runtime peaks, quiet behavior and ownership; LOWBAR records delivery and latency on constrained links. Hosts, scenarios, memory profiles, fixture generations and acceptance rules changed. The percentages below cannot be multiplied or added into an overall app speedup.

## Largest demonstrated effects

- **Gigabit-class throughput through adaptive window resizing:** the program achieved roughly **939–943 Mbps of useful TCP payload on a 1-Gbps test path**, rather than merely forecasting higher ceilings. ACK-residence sizing recovered **202.752→958.259–958.362 model Mbps**, and path-change recovery restored **4.055→958.075 model Mbps**. Window resizing remains enabled; disabling extra application pacing in October did not remove it. See the dedicated milestone below.
- **Less work per packet:** provider grouping cut a 16-packet Transfer drain's time by **64.4%**, allocated bytes by **79.3%**, and allocation count by **65.3%**. Ready-only H1 batching improved local ACK-sized throughput **85.2%** without adding a batching delay.
- **Cheaper H1 carriers:** the implemented H1+ helper improved local 1,200-byte upload/download/duplex throughput **32.35% / 18.68% / 36.31%** in the paired cohort. These are carrier benchmarks, not equivalent improvements in Fast.com or battery life.
- **Less retained memory:** the shared provider UDP lifecycle reduced local SDK host runtime **27.9→25.8 MiB** and goroutines **621→242**. Clearing a stale TCP reader reference reduced one matched SDK race diagnostic's warm-heap spread **7.86→0.54 MiB**.
- **Recovery and browsing:** a controlled August H1 path delivered Fast.com **38/41/52 Mbps**, with a **17.60 MiB** active runtime peak. October pacing and service-recovery work subsequently produced passing 100-page synthetic runs, but the broader cold-connection/device pause investigation remains open.
- **Important limit:** the complete current physical **32 MiB iOS-profile / 64 MiB Android-profile** matrix and the full correctness/PERF campaign are not qualified. Individual memory passes or fast public exits do not establish that qualification.

## Gigabit throughput milestone: adaptive window resizing

**Achieving gigabit-class delivery was a major result of this research.** The work replaced a fixed-window bottleneck with delivery-sized windows and repaired the feedback/ACK behavior needed to keep a fast path full. It deserves to stand alongside H1+, batching and memory retention as a headline improvement, rather than appear only as an intermediate 68→85-Mbps experiment.

The result was more than an arithmetic prediction. The local TCP-path instrument—real loopback kernel origin, provider NAT, Transfer and gVisor application/TUN sockets—recorded sustained **939–942 Mbps** after startup in its 100-ms, eight-flow upload check. Final larger-buffer capacity controls recorded approximately **942–943 Mbps**, including the 100-ms single-flow upload. This is gigabit-class useful payload on the instrument's 1-Gbps path; protocol overhead and the payload accounting explain why these readings are below 1,000 Mbps. It is host-local TCP-path evidence, not a claim that every mobile device or public provider supplies gigabit Internet service. [S22]

### What changed

- **Replace the fixed window with an adaptive one.** Qualified delivered-byte/rate and feedback-residence evidence can grow the send window; the receiver advertises usable credit. Peer permission, explicit endpoint limits and shared memory admission remain hard bounds. A larger permitted window is not an eager allocation of that many bytes.
- **Preserve older providers.** A peer without a receive-window advertisement keeps the historical opening-window behavior; missing compression metadata retains the legacy 10-ms assumption. Resizing cannot grant permission beyond the peer or shared budget.
- **Account for ACK compression in sizing.** A fast path can still starve if the window covers propagation but not the delayed/compressed ACK interval. Sizing includes the relevant feedback residence while retransmission timing keeps its separate RTT semantics.
- **Recover after path-quality changes.** Ordinary evidence grows the learned window; a bounded quality-change generation permits replacement using fresh service and RTT evidence. SDK, Android, Apple, Windows and Linux hooks propagate the quality event. Repeated noise does not repeatedly renew the generation.
- **Keep window sizing separate from pacing.** Delivery-window resizing is still the shipping default (`WindowSizingFromDelivery`). The extra application byte-pacing layer was subsequently disabled to address browsing stalls. That later correction did **not** discard the window-resizing work or its receiver/memory guards.

## Research methods used

The program moved from exploratory tuning toward tests that distinguish competing causes:

- **Profile and measure the actual owners.** Go allocation/heap/goroutine investigations, packet-root accounting, queue high-water/drop counters, GC cycles/CPU and process measurements distinguished live payload, free-list retention, allocator slack, goroutine topology and stale references. Memory measurements kept heap, Go runtime, RSS and iOS kernel footprint separate. Source-level capacity arithmetic was checked against actual retained roots.
- **Find the limiting boundary, not merely the slow request.** Packet/ACK lineage, per-lane resend and cumulative/selective ACK counters, TCP/TLS/request timing, provider selection receipts and browser CDP/NavigationTiming located pauses before admission, during carrier/queue transit, at TCP establishment or in DNS. Missing instrumentation was classified as unknown, not proof of a zero or a stall.
- **Reproduce mechanisms deterministically.** Fixed clocks (`synctest`), held queues/receipts, exact refused admissions, controlled ACK/body-release barriers, loss injection and old-code counterfactuals pinned the causal invariant. Tests covered cancellation, sequence wrap, SYN/FIN/RST generations, legacy peers, sibling flows, zero/shared budgets and returned ownership. Normal and race modes catch different failure classes.
- **Use ablations and negative controls.** Change only pacing, only drains, only window sizing, only allocation ownership or only a buffer rule. Keep failed candidates and compare against old behavior. Examples include the failed drain-only browsing treatment, the root-depth increase that removed drops without increasing speed, and the socket change that helped a synthetic floor but hurt a realistic profile.
- **Measure several layers.** Microbenchmarks isolate cost/op and allocations; real localhost TCP/TLS and native RPC measure carrier work; full-TUN/provider simulators test composition under seeded bandwidth/RTT/jitter/loss/MTU/outage; Android browser runs exercise Wikipedia/Fast.com on Wi-Fi/cellular. None substitutes for the next layer. Linux kernel/socket conclusions require the corresponding Linux fixture.
- **Balance comparisons and quantify noise.** Same-binary switches, immutable source overlays, alternating/interleaved fresh-process blocks and baseline/candidate/candidate/baseline order reduce drift. A/A estimates establish detectable effects. Paired ratios, bootstrap intervals, exact tests and multiplicity correction are used where the cohort supports them; pilots and one-off passes remain explicitly weaker evidence.
- **Treat evidence validity as part of the test.** Preserve useful-byte hashes, exact attempt counts, failed/calibration-invalid rows, source/toolchain identities and raw artifacts. Join background workers and process groups before final memory accounting. Do not force GC to create a steady-memory pass, silently extend deadlines, discard bad providers after seeing results, or compare only successful tail samples. CPU reductions are energy hypotheses until actual energy is measured.

## What changed in the research instruments

The improvements in test coverage are substantial even where performance remains open:

1. **LOWBAR:** constrained-rate, MTU, loss, mixed-route and fixed-offer cases now check useful delivery, retransmission/wire cost and finite queues, rather than only clean-path bulk speed.
2. **PERFVAR:** scenario identity and A/A noise matter; every scheduled attempt and failed/calibration-invalid arm remains in the record. Five repeats cannot automatically establish a small gain. Loaded-probe cohorts must settle through their own deadlines rather than be silently discarded when bulk completes.
3. **MEMSTEADY:** Go soft limits and logical credit are not observed runtime caps. Startup, traffic, quiet, drain and joined teardown all count. The current iOS surrogate is tested on allowlisted Android phones at **32 MiB**, while normal Android is independently tested at **64 MiB**. A later low sample cannot erase an earlier breach.
4. **Browsing:** tests evolved from sequential/easy pages to 100 pages of 50–300 KiB with real HTML/assets, unfinished bodies and overlapping navigation, then actual SDK/public-provider recovery and verified inner TLS/H2. Those fixture generations are distinct measurements, not interchangeable score rows.

There is no native-iPhone evidence establishing the requested extension lifetime `phys_footprint <50 MiB`. Android/host tests cannot supply that conclusion.
